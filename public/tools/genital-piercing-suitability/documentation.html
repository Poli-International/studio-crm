What it is for

The Genital Piercing Anatomy Suitability Checker is an educational reference covering fourteen female, male, and shared genital piercing placements. It explains required tissue structures, manual examination checks, starting jewellery parameters, and reasons a piercer may decline a placement. A private worksheet helps users prepare questions for an in-person studio consultation.

Who it is for

  • Adult clients (18+): Individuals researching genital piercings who want to understand anatomical requirements before an in-person consultation.
  • Professional body piercers and studio staff: Practitioners who need clear terminology to explain suitability criteria and anatomical limitations to clients.

How to use it

1. Switching the interface language

  1. Click the Language dropdown in the top bar.
  2. Select English, French, Italian, German, Spanish, Dutch, or Portuguese.
  3. The interface translates all labels, assessment notes, and diagram captions immediately.

2. Reviewing the age and consent notice

  1. Read the top banner: Adult educational reference (18+). Piercers require government identification and informed adult consent prior to consultation.
  2. Confirm you meet the legal adult age requirement before booking an appointment.

3. Searching by placement name or common term

  1. Click the search input displaying Search placement or common name (e.g. VCH, clit hood, PA, guiche)....
  2. Type an anatomical term or common alias such as "VCH", "clit hood", "PA", or "frenum".
  3. Click the Clear search button to reset the input and restore all placements.

4. Filtering by anatomical category

  1. Open the Filter by anatomy dropdown menu.
  2. Select All placements (14), Female anatomy placements, Male anatomy placements, or Shared / perineal placements.
  3. Use Select placement to display the chosen piercing.

5. Reviewing anatomical requirements and assessment criteria

  1. Read Anatomy Required & Why for structural landmarks.
  2. Review In-Person Assessment (What a Piercer Examines) for manual examination checks.
  3. Check Common Reasons a Piercer Says No for anatomical contraindications.
  4. Note Typical healing range and Starting jewellery facts for initial expectations.
  5. Review the four prompts under Questions to Ask Your Piercer to discuss during your appointment.

6. Viewing schematic cross-section diagrams

  1. Click Show schematic diagram under the placement overview.
  2. Review the cross-sectional vector diagram showing the tissue path and exit points.
  3. Click Hide schematic diagram to collapse the graphic when reading in public.

7. Using the private consultation worksheet

  1. Scroll down to the consultation worksheet section.
  2. Review the prompts for Comfort with in-person physical assessment and Time & activity flexibility for healing.
  3. Enter personal notes into the textarea labelled My private notes & questions for consultation.
  4. Check Save private notes in this browser to keep notes on this device across visits.
  5. Click Clear all private notes at any time to clear the textarea and local storage.

8. Generating a printable consultation sheet

  1. Click Print Consultation Sheet at the bottom of the worksheet.
  2. Review the greyscale summary displaying the placement, assessment points, questions, and notes.
  3. Print the document or choose "Save as PDF" in your browser print dialogue.

9. Reviewing shared clinical reasons for refusal

  1. Scroll to the clinical reference section at the bottom of the page.
  2. Read the five shared reasons piercers decline placements: insufficient tissue depth, direct nerve proximity, friction pressure points, surgical scarring, and active dermatological irritation.

Where your data lives

Your data stays on your personal device inside your browser:

  • Zero server transmission: The tool makes no network requests and never transmits notes or search terms to Poli International or external servers.
  • Volatile by default: If Save private notes in this browser is unchecked, notes exist only in browser memory and clear when the tab closes or refreshes.
  • Opt-in local storage: If Save private notes in this browser is checked, text is stored in local storage under the key poli_genital_suitability_notes.
  • Clearing data: Clicking Clear all private notes deletes the local storage key and clears the input. Clearing browser cache and cookies also removes it.
  • Export contents: The printed sheet contains only the active placement name, the four consultation questions, and your private notes.

Printing and exporting

  • Dedicated print styles: Clicking Print Consultation Sheet activates print styles that hide navigation, search inputs, language selectors, and action buttons.
  • High contrast format: The summary formats into clean greyscale suitable for standard letter or A4 printing.
  • PDF generation: Use your browser print dialogue to send output directly to a printer or save it as a local PDF document.

Questions and answers

Can I determine my suitability for a genital piercing online?

No online tool or photograph can determine whether your anatomy suits a genital piercing. A piercer must physically palpate tissue to evaluate depth, elasticity, mobility, and vascular positioning. Final suitability is only determined during an in-person physical consultation at a professional studio.

What is the Q-tip test for a vertical clitoral hood (VCH) piercing?

The Q-tip test is a manual check used by piercers to confirm whether the clitoral hood has sufficient depth for a VCH piercing. The piercer gently inserts the cotton tip of a sterile swab under the hood to ensure a barbell can sit without pressing against the clitoral glans. If the swab cannot insert freely or the hood lacks enough depth, the anatomy cannot support a vertical placement.

Why does a Prince Albert piercing require a heavy starting wire gauge?

A Prince Albert piercing requires a starting gauge of at least 12 gauge (2.0 mm) or 10 gauge (2.4 mm) to prevent the cheese-cutter effect. Thin wire gauges concentrate tension onto a small area of delicate urethral tissue, risking migration or tearing. Heavy gauge wire distributes pressure over a broader contact area to ensure stable healing.

Why do piercers frequently refuse Christina piercings?

A Christina piercing is a surface piercing through the pubic mound exiting at the top of the clitoral hood. Piercers refuse this placement if a client lacks a distinct pubic tissue crease to anchor the bottom barbell bend securely. Without sufficient tissue depth and a natural pocket, daily friction from walking and clothing causes high rejection rates.

Can an uncircumcised person receive a Dydoe piercing?

Dydoe piercings pass through the coronal ridge of the glans and are rarely recommended for uncircumcised individuals. The natural foreskin covers the coronal ridge, trapping moisture, secretions, and bacteria against the healing wound. Movement from the foreskin also applies persistent lateral friction to the jewelry, creating chronic irritation and migration.

What is the anatomical difference between an Apadravya and an Ampallang?

An Apadravya passes vertically through the center of the glans, entering through the underside urethra and exiting the top surface. An Ampallang passes horizontally through the glans from side to side, crossing through the urethra midway. Both are advanced trans-glans procedures that require substantial tissue volume and experienced technique.

Limits

This tool cannot replace a physical examination. It cannot palpate tissue elasticity, measure flaccid versus erect tissue variance, detect sub-surface vascular structures, assess nerve proximity, or evaluate individual healing capacity.

Only an experienced professional body piercer, conducting an in-person physical consultation in a sterile studio setting, can evaluate your anatomy and determine suitability. If your piercer concludes that your anatomy cannot support a specific placement, accept their professional judgment and discuss safer anatomical alternatives.
