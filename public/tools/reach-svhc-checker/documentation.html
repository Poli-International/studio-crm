REACH SVHC Pigment Checker: user guide

Screen tattoo ink, body jewelry, and studio product ingredients against a curated body-art subset of the ECHA SVHC Candidate List by substance name and CAS number.

What it is for

The REACH SVHC Pigment Checker screens chemical names, commercial aliases, and Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) registry numbers against a curated subset of Substances of Very High Concern (SVHCs). The European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) manages the Candidate List under Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 (REACH). When a substance enters the Candidate List, EU suppliers must disclose its presence above 0.1 percent weight by weight (w/w).

This tool compares single queries or Safety Data Sheet (SDS) composition blocks against 51 curated body-art entries from the official 241-substance ECHA snapshot (27 June 2024), presenting hazard classifications, studio uses, and match confidence tiers.

Who it is for

  • Tattoo artists and permanent makeup (PMU) technicians checking ink formulations and Safety Data Sheets before introducing new batches.
  • Body piercers and jewelry buyers screening alloys, plasticizers, and polymer components for restricted heavy metals or Candidate List additives.
  • Studio owners and hygiene managers organizing chemical inventories and maintaining studio due-diligence records.
  • Distributors and importers auditing foreign manufacturer SDS Section 3 sheets against Candidate List entries before commercial dispatch.

How to use it

Theme and language controls

  1. Locate the top utility bar above the tool title.
  2. Click Toggle color theme to switch between dark and light modes.
  3. Open the Language selector to choose English, Deutsch, Español, Français, Italiano, Português, or Nederlands. All interface labels, alerts, hazard classifications, and substance profiles update immediately.

Inspecting coverage details

  1. In the header below the title, click Coverage details: 51 curated body-art substances.
  2. A modal dialog titled SVHC Coverage & Screening Scope opens over the page.
  3. Review the substance breakdown across five body-art categories:
  • Heavy metals & metal compounds (lead, cadmium, hexavalent chromium, cobalt salts, nickel)
  • Primary aromatic amines (PAAs) and azo cleavage precursors
  • Phthalate plasticizers found in synthetic polymers and packaging
  • Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) associated with Carbon Black (CI 77266)
  • Industrial solvents, preservatives, and glycol ethers
  1. Click Close modal or click outside the dialog box to return to the workspace.

Searching a single ingredient

  1. Click the tab labeled Search ingredient.
  2. Click inside the input field below Ingredient name or CAS number. The placeholder displays e.g. Nickel, 7440-02-0, Cobalt, DEHP, CI 77491….
  3. Type an ingredient name (such as Nickel), an alias (such as DBP), or a CAS number (such as 7440-02-0).
  4. Click Check or press Enter.
  5. If the field is blank, the tool displays an alert: Please enter an ingredient name or CAS number to check.
  6. Inspect the resulting card:
  • If flagged, an alert card expands showing the substance name, CAS number, hazard badge, risk level (High risk, Med risk, or Low risk), and match confidence (High confidence, Med confidence, or Partial match).
  • If not flagged, a confirmation card displays the query with the notice Not found in the 51 curated body-art SVHC substances. Verify against the official ECHA Candidate List.

Scanning a bulk SDS composition block

  1. Click the tab labeled Paste SDS block.
  2. Copy Section 3 (Composition / Information on Ingredients) from your product Safety Data Sheet.
  3. Paste the text into the box beneath Ingredient list or SDS Section 3.
  4. Click Scan for SVHCs.
  5. If empty, the tool displays an alert: Please paste an ingredient list or SDS composition block before scanning.
  6. Review the summary bar:
  • Indicators display SVHC flagged counts alongside not in curated list counts.
  • If SVHCs are identified, a warning banner advises you to review supplier documentation.
  • If no SVHCs are found, an informational banner confirms: No SVHC substances from the curated body-art list were detected. Always verify against the official ECHA list for legally binding information.
  1. Review flagged cards under Flagged substances ({count}), followed by unflagged items under Not in curated SVHC list ({count}).
  2. To clear your text block and reset the scan view, click Clear.

Reading substance cards and cross-tool links

  1. Click any flagged card header to expand or collapse technical fields.
  2. Read the structured properties:
  • Hazard classification: Regulatory hazard designation.
  • EC number: European Community inventory number.
  • CAS number(s): Chemical Abstracts Service registry numbers.
  • Match confidence: Exact match condition explaining whether the hit occurred by CAS number, standard chemical name, commercial alias, or word boundary.
  • Found in body art products: Documented uses such as piercing alloys, tattoo pigments, or packaging.
  • Also known as: Common industry synonyms and chemical aliases.
  1. Follow workflow links when available:
  • For nickel hits, click Calculate nickel release rate under EN 1811 in the Nickel Release Calculator to open https://poliinternational.com/nickel-release-calculator/ in the top window.
  • For metallic compounds, click Verify metal alloy specifications in the Material Certification Checker to open https://poliinternational.com/material-certification-checker/ in the top window.
  • For ink pigments, amines, and plasticizers, click Decode pigments and restrictions in the Ink Ingredient Decoder to open https://poliinternational.com/ink-ingredient-decoder/ in the top window.
  1. Access primary regulatory sources directly through 🔗 View on ECHA or 📋 Full SVHC List.

What it does not do

  • It does not test for REACH Annex XVII Entry 75 concentration limits. Entry 75 sets concentration limits on tattoo inks and permanent makeup. To analyze ink formulations and specific pigment restrictions, use the Poli International Ink Ingredient Decoder.
  • It does not measure nickel release rates for jewelry insertion. REACH Annex XVII Entry 27 limits nickel migration from post assemblies to 0.2 micrograms per square centimetre per week. To compute migration test results under standard EN 1811, use the Poli International Nickel Release Calculator.
  • It does not verify mill certificates or standard alloy specifications. To compare mill test reports against surgical implant specifications such as ASTM F-136 titanium or ASTM F-138 stainless steel, use the Poli International Material Certification Checker.
  • It does not calculate piercing fee structures or shop margins. To benchmark procedure pricing and calculate retail jewelry margins, use the Poli International Studio Pricing Benchmark.
  • It does not perform chemical laboratory assay testing. The tool matches text strings and CAS codes against a static database. It cannot detect unlisted impurities or fraudulent declarations.

Where your data lives

  • Local browser execution: Every search query and pasted SDS block is evaluated directly inside your web browser on your local device.
  • Zero network transmission: The application makes no external API calls, tracking queries, or background server requests. Pasted formulation text is never transmitted across the network.
  • Persistence and clearing: The tool does not store formulation data in database tables. Refreshing the browser tab, navigating away, or clicking Clear instantly purges pasted text and screening results from memory.
  • Local storage settings: Only visual interface settings (such as light or dark theme) are stored in client-side memory.

Printing and exporting

  • Browser print stylesheet: You can print screening results or save them as a clean PDF using your browser print command (Ctrl+P or Cmd+P).
  • Print layout optimization: The stylesheet removes navigation tabs, buttons, search inputs, and modal backgrounds when printing.
  • Audit header and footer: Printed records include the search date, the curated snapshot designation (51 of 253 substances, 27 June 2024 snapshot), and the tool URL (https://poliinternational.com/tools/reach-svhc-checker/index.html) for studio compliance records.

Questions and answers

What is an SVHC under EU REACH regulation?

An SVHC is a Substance of Very High Concern identified under Article 57 of European Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 (REACH). These substances possess hazardous profiles including Carcinogenic, Mutagenic, or Reprotoxic properties (CMR Category 1A or 1B), Persistent, Bioaccumulative, and Toxic properties (PBT), very Persistent and very Bioaccumulative properties (vPvB), or equivalent concerns such as endocrine disruption. Once approved, ECHA places them on the official Candidate List for eventual authorization.

Does a negative screening result mean my tattoo ink fully complies with REACH?

No, a negative screening result indicates only that none of the 51 curated body-art SVHC substances were identified in your search query or pasted composition text. Full compliance with European REACH rules for tattoo inks also requires meeting the strict concentration thresholds in REACH Annex XVII Entry 75, which regulates thousands of individual substances, azo dye cleavage precursors, and specific banned pigments. Always cross-reference complete supplier Safety Data Sheets.

Why does this tool cover 51 substances instead of all 253 on the official ECHA list?

The official ECHA Candidate List contains hundreds of industrial intermediates, specialized aerospace compounds, and manufacturing reagents that have no documented presence in body-art products. This tool focuses on a curated subset of 51 substances with established history or known contamination risk in tattoo inks, permanent makeup, piercing alloys, synthetic body jewelry polymers, packaging plasticizers, or studio cleaning consumables.

How does the bulk SDS scanner extract chemical ingredients?

The bulk scanner parses text blocks using regular expressions that identify standard Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) registry numbers formatted as two to seven digits, a hyphen, two digits, a hyphen, and a single check digit. It also strips percentage concentration figures, separates ingredients by commas, semicolons, or line breaks, and matches remaining chemical names and aliases against its indexed substance database.

What should a studio do if an ingredient is flagged as an SVHC?

If an ingredient is flagged, check Section 3 of your supplier Safety Data Sheet to determine its stated concentration. Under European REACH Article 33, suppliers must notify recipients if an SVHC exceeds 0.1 percent weight by weight (w/w). If the substance is present above regulatory limits or restricted under REACH Annex XVII, contact your supplier immediately for technical clarification and quarantine the stock until full compliance is confirmed in writing.

What is the difference between an SVHC Candidate List substance and an Annex XVII restriction?

The Candidate List identifies candidate substances under evaluation for authorization under REACH Title VII, triggering immediate disclosure duties when present above 0.1 percent w/w. Annex XVII represents REACH Title VIII restrictions that legally prohibit or limit the manufacture, marketing, or use of specific substances in consumer products regardless of authorization status, such as Entry 27 for nickel release in jewelry and Entry 75 for chemical substances in tattoo inks.

Limits

  • Static curated snapshot: The database represents a curated subset of 51 substances drawn from the ECHA Candidate List release of 27 June 2024. If the snapshot age exceeds six months without an updated revision, a staleness notice appears warning users to verify recent additions on the official ECHA website.
  • No detection of unlisted constituents: The tool only inspects the text you provide. It cannot discover unlisted chemical constituents, undeclared carrier solvents, or secret formulation changes that a manufacturer omitted from the Safety Data Sheet.
  • Text recognition variations: Irregular trade names, spelling errors, or complex chemical salt variants not captured in the synonym dictionary may return a non-flagged result. When in doubt, search by exact CAS registry number rather than proprietary trade name.
  • Legal authority: This tool provides rapid screening assistance for body-art professionals but does not provide legal advice or formal compliance certification. Regulatory enforcement decisions rest exclusively with national competent authorities, chemical inspection bodies, and testing laboratories performing accredited analytical spectrometry.
