Piercing & Tattoo Healing Tracker: Technical Documentation

Architecture, logic specifications, data schemas, and integration guide.

1. Architecture Overview

The Healing Tracker is a self-contained, client-side web application designed to compute and display stage-by-stage healing expectations for body piercings and tattoos. It runs without external network dependencies, server requirements, or tracking scripts.

  • HTML5: Semantic markup with accessible form controls and tabbed interfaces.
  • CSS3: Theme-driven stylesheets using CSS custom properties with real-time dark/light switching.
  • JavaScript: Modular scripts providing deterministic timeline calculation, symptom triage, and aftercare checklisting.
  • Local Storage: Client-side persistence for theme preferences.

2. Core Logic & Calculation

The healing engine calculates elapsed days using standard UTC day offsets: 

const procDate = new Date(date);
const today = new Date();
const daysElapsed = Math.max(0, Math.floor((today - procDate) / (1000 * 60 * 60 * 24)));

Symptom Triage Logic

Symptoms are categorized across three severity thresholds:

  • Concerning: Triggered when symptoms like persistent bleeding, spreading red streaks, or foul discharge are reported. Directs immediate medical/professional escalation.
  • Monitor Closely: Triggered for moderate swelling, prolonged redness, or localized warmth. Suggests active observation within 24–48 hours.
  • Normal: Expected healing responses including clear plasma weeping (whitish lymph drying to crust), mild tenderness, and dry crusting.

Healing Log & Advanced Analysis Specifications

  • Photo Comparison: Allows side-by-side milestone comparison between Day 0 baseline and any logged entry using an interactive split-view draggable divider. Implemented in renderPhotoComparison().
  • Symptom Trends & Series Sentences: Visualizes redness, swelling, tenderness, and discharge severity over time via pure SVG polyline charts and computes trajectory summary sentences. Implemented in renderSymptomTrends() and generateSymptomSentences().
  • Direction-Change Detection: Identifies sustained multi-day reversals from an improving trajectory. Implemented in detectDirectionChange() with a strict threshold requirement of 3 consecutive worsening entries (or >= 48 hours of sustained increase) to eliminate false alarms from transient daily friction while ensuring timely studio review.
  • What Changed Trigger: Automatically prompts for physical triggers (snagged, slept on it, new jewellery, changed product, swimming, gym workout, nearby piercing) whenever symptoms worsen compared to the preceding entry. Implemented in evaluateWhatChangedTrigger(). Records the cause without diagnosing.
  • Backup & Restore: Self-contained single-file JSON export and import with embedded base64 milestone imagery for cross-device migration. Implemented in exportBackupData() and importBackupData().

Touch-Up Longevity & Fading Engine

The longevity calculator determines the optimal touch-up horizon using empirical modifier multipliers:

  • Base Longevity: Derived from style density (e.g. 10–15 years for tribal/blackwork down to 3–5 years for fine line or watercolor).
  • Placement Multiplier: Adjusts for friction and epidermal turnover (Covered: 1.1x, Occasionally exposed: 1.0x, Frequently exposed: 0.8x, High friction: 0.5x).
  • Pigment Longevity Hierarchy: Accounts for differing chemical light-fastness (Black > Dark Blue > Green/Purple > Red > Yellow > Orange > Pink > White).
  • Care & UV Modifiers: Applies empirical modifiers for daily moisturization and broad-spectrum SPF 50+ protection.

3. Frequently Asked Questions & Clinical Facts

Evidence-based clinical clarification on materials, lymph weeping, and long-term biocompatibility.

Q: Is titanium the only safe material for initial piercing jewellery?

Fact: No. While implant-grade titanium (such as ASTM F-136 or ISO 5832-3) is an exceptional, highly inert material widely recommended for fresh piercings, it is not the only safe option. Other established, biocompatible materials suitable for initial healing include implant-grade surgical steel (such as ASTM F-138), biocompatible niobium (ASTM B392), solid 14k or 18k nickel-free gold, and BioFlex® body jewelry, a PP-R random copolymer whose resin holds USP Class VI. Safe initial jewellery requires proven biocompatibility, non-toxicity, and compliance with strict nickel release limits, rather than a single exclusive material.

Q: Is BioFlex® body jewelry only intended for short-term or temporary wear?

Fact: No. BioFlex® body jewelry is made from a PP-R (polypropylene random copolymer). The resin holds USP Class VI and meets Ph. Eur. 3.2.2, and the material is certified as passing the ISO 10993 biological tests. It is suitable for long-term wear as well as initial piercings, can be steam sterilised in an autoclave, and its flexibility reduces pressure on the tissue. Note on material distinction: BioFlex® is not PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene). PTFE is a fluoropolymer machined from rod, with different mechanical and threading properties; BioFlex® body jewelry is a PP-R copolymer made in one piece, with no joints or glued parts. Neither material is restricted to short-term wear when used appropriately.

Q: My piercing has clear or whitish crusty fluid. Does this mean it is infected?

Fact: No. Clear, pale whitish, or straw-coloured fluid that dries into crust ("crusties") is lymphatic fluid (lymph) mixed with dead cellular matter. Lymph production is a completely normal, healthy component of the body's wound-healing response across all piercing stages. It is not pus and is not a sign of infection. Signs of actual infection differ distinctly: thick opaque yellow or dark green pus, hot radiating warmth, spreading dark redness, throbbing pain, or systemic fever.

5. Privacy & Compliance

All calculations are performed entirely within the user's browser. No personal dates, health symptom selections, or procedure details are ever transmitted to any remote server or stored in cookies.
